In this meeting, organized by [Patti Digitali Pavesi](https://www.facebook.com/people/Patti-Digitali-Pavesi/61559655394908/) during the two-day event [Pavia Città del Futuro](https://www.unipv.news/eventi/citta-del-futuro-il-domani-che-sogniamo-0), the part that struck me most was the growing consensus, fueled by a number of statistical researches and a now consolidated orientation of the community of pediatricians and psychologists, of how **the use without a correct educational context of social networks and smartphones by minors is slowly, but inexorably, changing their way of responding and interacting with the world**, and also their ability to learn.

My speech focused on the need, as parents, to get involved to [know the possibilities offered by parental control tools](/resources/family-link-android-guide) and use them as elements to **establish a dialogue with our children around good digital wellbeing practices**. Because first a **shared agreement on how and what to explore digitally** is necessary, and only later use parental control to make parents and children confident that these common rules are respected.

Furthermore, my advice was to **create rules that are always a bit more "permissive" than the agreements made**. For example, if the decision is to use the smartphone for half an hour a day, set the usage limit to 40 minutes, so that the boy/girl is empowered to respect the agreement on their own, and not to passively wait for the device block. If they manage to do so, maybe the next day they receive 10 extra minutes of "bonus" usage.

The various questions that emerged from the audience confirmed the **fundamental role of the school in proper digital and technology use education**. Both for parents, and for their children. Correctly managing the electronic register, being sure that even teachers do not use their smartphones in class, and the courage that the school should have in proposing activities aimed at all families, especially those less attentive to these issues, as it is one of the few actors able to reach and sensitize them.

There were also questions about the role of artificial intelligence in all this, surrounded by a great deal of concern. But I will come back to this topic calmly another time.

For now, I am left with a beautiful memory of a classroom with still some empty seats (as always happens a bit at these events) but certainly with a truly stimulating "density of minds" and points of view.
