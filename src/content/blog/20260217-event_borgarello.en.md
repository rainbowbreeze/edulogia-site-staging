In educating for mindful digital use, the **dialogue between children and parents is fundamental**. While the former "learn by doing", the latter can "learn by sharing". And this was the approach chosen for the event organized in Borgarello by [Patti Digitali Pavesi](https://www.facebook.com/p/Patti-Digitali-Pavesi-61559655394908/) and [CoderDojo Pavia](https://ctrlalt.museum), where I presented a session on Parental Control, and how to make an Android smartphone safer using [Google Family Link](https://families.google/familylink/).

The guiding thread of the day's content, in my opinion, was "Knowing so as not to demonize".
- Giulia dall'Aglio, from [Tecnologia Familiare](https://tecnologiafamiliare.it/) talked about how the "right" video games can be a valid tool to support education
- The CoderDojo activities demonstrated how computers, if used correctly, help develop computational thinking and active problem solving
- I talked about how the correct [configuration of a smartphone via parental control](/resources/family-link-android-guide) can help minors in their journey of discovering the digital world, and even become a lever for dialogue to decide, parents and children together, how to manage this exploration

Consistent with past experiences were also the observations of the many parents present (along with some teachers):
- The great fear of making mistakes given the complexity of this world
- The difficulty of managing the demands of their children, often induced by social pressure and the emulation of other friends, rather than by a real need
- The difficulty of finding validation in the attention towards correct digital education in other family groups they meet at school, in sports activities, or simply in the group of friends
- The scarcity of reliable and comprehensive material that can support parents in their needs as educators

In short. If on one hand **the speakers shared that "it is possible"**, on the other **the families replied with a "Yes, but it's difficult"**. Education is an intertwined network of initiatives. And there is still much to do.
