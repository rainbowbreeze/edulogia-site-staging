# Google Family Link Guide for Android

## Setting specific app usage limits
In addition to setting a daily usage limit for your child's phone (or no limit), you can define a specific usage time for each app.

* **No app limit**: standard phone time limits apply. The app can be used each day for the maximum time your child is allowed to use their phone. This is the default setting for all apps, allowing your child to independently manage the time they have available.
* **Time limit**: the app can be used for a maximum amount of time. For example, your child can access their favorite game for 15 minutes, while still being able to use the phone for an hour a day (or having unlimited phone use).
* **Always allow**: the app can always be used and will not count towards the phone's time limit. For example, your child can always use the camera app, or the math practice app, for as long as they want.

### How to proceed:
1. Open the Family Link app on your parent phone, and select the "Screen time" tab. Here you can see how much time your child has already spent on the phone.
2. Select "Time limits".
3. Select "App limits".
4. In this list, you can check all the set app limits.
5. Select an app to modify it.
6. Here you can first allow the app to be used on your child's phone by selecting "Allow".
   * With "No app limit", the same phone time restrictions apply to the app.
   * With "Set limit", you can set the maximum daily usage time for the app (e.g., 15 minutes a day).
   * Choose "Always allow" to never block access to the app (e.g., for the Camera).
7. If you want more information about the app, simply select "App details". You will find a range of information, including the recommended minimum age (e.g., PEGI 3).
8. Returning to the "App limits" list, the icon next to the app will reflect the new setting (e.g., always allow).

Also from the time limits screen, you can quickly check which apps have specific usage limits and which have unlimited usage time.

**What happens on your child's phone:**
If an app has a usage time limit, a notification initially appears when the maximum time is about to be reached. Then the app is automatically closed when the limit is reached, and its icon turns gray. If they try to launch it, a message warns that the daily limit has been reached and they must wait another day to use it again.

## Setting screen-free time (Downtime)
To reduce distractions at night or during school hours, you can set your child's device so that it cannot be used during a certain time frame, or can only be used with certain always-allowed apps. As with time limits, it's always possible for your child to receive calls and make emergency calls (if the phone has a SIM).

"Downtime" defines the time frame in which your child **cannot** use their phone/tablet. The "Time limit" defines the maximum amount of time they can use it within the allowed time frame. 

Using these two settings in combination, you can, for example, set that on weekdays your child can use the phone between snack time and dinner, but for a maximum of 45 minutes.

### How to proceed:
1. To set a downtime, choose "Screen time", and then "Schedules".
2. On the main management screen, you can enable and disable "Downtime", check the setting for the current day, modify the "Weekly schedule", and decide if your child can still access certain apps.
3. Enable "Downtime" and select "Weekly schedule".
4. Here you can check what time frame each day your child cannot use the phone (e.g., from 8:00 PM to 5:30 PM for weekdays, and start 8:00 PM - end 10:00 AM for weekends).
5. By selecting a day of the week, you can manage the specific downtime for that day, or disable it completely.
6. If you select "Tonight" on the main management screen, you can set a time limit that only applies to the current day, without modifying the weekly schedule (useful for exceptions).
7. If you choose "Allowed apps", you can decide whether apps that DO NOT have a time limit can also be used while the phone is in its downtime. You can check which apps these are and potentially change their limits.
8. If you disable "Always allow apps", your child will only be able to receive and make emergency calls during the downtime.

## Setting school time
Like downtime, school time allows you to assign a time frame where the device is locked to encourage studying or resting. Your child will receive a notification when the time frame is about to end and when the device is locked. From that moment, access to apps and notifications will be blocked, but they can always handle emergency calls.
