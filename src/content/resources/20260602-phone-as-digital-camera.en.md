# A safe digital camera for our children

An old Android phone tucked away in a drawer can become a simple and safe digital camera to leave in our children's hands, letting them have fun taking pictures and reviewing them whenever they like.  
There is no need for a SIM card, all other apps are disabled, and the photos are not uploaded online.
This way, even young children can play at taking pictures and looking at them, at home or outside, without any privacy risks.

# What to do, in brief

You start with a working Family Link family group setup. Here you can find [the complete step-by-step guide](/resources/family-link-android-guide). You need to have configured at least one parent account, a child account to manage, and an Android phone set up with the child's account.

Then:
- Install a photo gallery app that does not upload images online, such as [Google Gallery](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.photosgo) or [Right Gallery](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.goodwy.gallery).
- From Family Link, disable all apps except the camera and the photo gallery app.
- In necessary, from Family Link set up a bedtime schedule so that the phone can only be used during the day.
- If necessary, still from Family Link, set a maximum daily usage time to prevent the camera or gallery from being used for too long during the day.

# The detailed steps to follow

I won't repeat here how to create a family group on Family Link, create a child account, and set up an Android phone with the child's account. It's all already documented in the [Family Link guide I created](/resources/family-link-android-guide), with step-by-step instructions.

Many phones have a pre-installed gallery app that often connects to the Internet to back up photos to the cloud, create stickers, use AI editing, and other things. We completely avoid these apps by installing something much simpler and safer, such as [Google Gallery](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.photosgo) or [Right Gallery](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.goodwy.gallery). Both do not need the internet to work, have no ads, do not ask to enable online photo backup or other annoyances, but simply offer the ability to view photos and little else. Exactly what we need.
- Launch the Play Store from the child's phone.
- Search for [Google Gallery](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.photosgo) or [Right Gallery](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.goodwy.gallery), making sure they are actually the right ones and not apps with similar names.
- Install one of the two.
- The Google Photos app is not suitable because it constantly asks to enable online photo backup and offers many advanced photo editing and management features, which could distract our budding photographers.

Configure the phone to launch only the camera and photo gallery apps.
- Open Family Link, go to the *Controls* tab, and then to *App limits*.
- For each app in the list, select it and disable the *Allow app* option.
- Leave enabled only the camera app and the gallery app chosen in the previous step.

If necessary, configure bedtime limits and maximum daily usage time.
- Still from Family Link, go to the *Screen time* tab, and then to *Schedules*.
- Enable *Downtime* and set the hours when the phone cannot be used.
- Go back to the *Screen time* tab and this time select *Daily limit*.
- Enable the *Daily limit* option and set how long the phone can be used each day.

Once these configurations are done, and having checked that the child's phone actually has all other apps disabled, you can finally hand it over to your children without fear, inviting them to take photos and tell you about their adventures.
